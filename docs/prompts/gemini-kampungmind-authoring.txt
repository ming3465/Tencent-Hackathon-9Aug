You are helping draft a dialogue matrix for Kampung SG, a cozy top-down
Singapore HDB-estate game for an Age Well social-good challenge.

The design thesis is that older adults are experts and contributors, never
patients. The campaign has three chapters:

1. Open the Way: Mr. Long cannot comfortably cross a broken doorstep. The
   player recruits any three neighbours, each contributing practical knowledge,
   and the block builds a ramp together.
2. A Place at the Table: the player gathers clues about Grandma Ros, enters her
   kitchen, and invites residents to a community cooking lesson she leads.
3. Hands Remember: the player reconnects Ben with a craftsman and completes a
   calm, no-failure weaving interaction.

Draft one compact personality-and-dialogue row for each resident below:

- Aunty Mei: community mentor; restores the garden; herbs or seating flowers.
- Uncle Ravi: social organiser; makes a welcoming noticeboard event.
- Mdm Siti: access expert; improves a sheltered daily route.
- Pak Yusof: practical fixer; inspects Mr. Long's doorstep.
- Coach Meng: volunteer organiser; arranges accessible community-centre seating.
- Uncle Seng: routine-driven connector; prepares a welcoming kopitiam table.
- Auntie Minah: provision-shop neighbour; organises an ingredient shelf and
  knows two clues about Grandma Ros.
- Wei Ling: newer resident; completes a keepsake table and invites younger
  neighbours.

For each row provide:

- three qualitative personality traits;
- community role and one specific area of expertise;
- one concise first greeting;
- one line offering the optional request;
- one reminder after "Maybe later" that keeps the route open;
- one memory reaction after the player helped them;
- one Chapter 2 invitation response;
- one reflection after their relevant chapter.

Constraints:

- Warm, concise spoken English natural to a multi-ethnic Singapore estate.
- Light local cadence is welcome, but avoid caricature, phonetic accents, and
  overusing "lah".
- Show expertise through concrete details.
- No medical, cognitive, dementia, diagnostic, prevention, or treatment claims.
- No helpless-elder framing, pity, infantilisation, scoring, failure, timer,
  pressure, punishment, or dark patterns.
- Do not imply that the player fixes people. Residents organise and contribute.
- "Maybe later" must never sound like rejection or close a route.
- Do not invent statistics, research findings, playtests, or outcomes.
- All lines must be suitable for deterministic offline selection; do not refer
  to a chatbot, model, or generated response.
- Use original wording and do not quote existing games, films, books, or songs.

Return a Markdown table only. This is a drafting pass: a human will review and
curate every line before any content ships.
