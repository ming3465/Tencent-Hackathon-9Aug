## 3. Input rules that make takes repeatable

These are not style notes. Each one is a known behaviour of the current build
and ignoring it will ruin a take.

1. **Interact with `E` in the world. Activate dialog buttons with `Enter`.**
   *(Corrected in rev-2 — rev-1 stated this incorrectly.)* The scene toggles
   `Space` capture with the world controls (`sandboxScene.ts:212-221`):
   * **World active** → `addCapture([SPACE])`. `Space` is `preventDefault`-ed
     at the window, so it activates no DOM button; it is also registered as an
     interact key (`sandboxScene.ts:304-307`), so it fires `E`'s action.
   * **Dialog open** → `removeCapture([SPACE])`. `Space` now **does** activate
     the focused choice button (this is asserted by the smoke check *"Space
     activates a focused dialogue choice"*).
   Both keys work, in different places. Use **`E` in the world, `Enter` in
   dialogs** anyway — one habit, no ambiguity, and it matches the SRT.
2. **`Enter` is a tap, never a hold.** Key repeat fires the choice and then
   closes the dialog before the response text can be read.
3. **Close overlays with `Escape`.**
4. **Never hold `E` while a dialog is open.** A latched `JustDown` will
   re-open the dialog the frame after you close it.
5. **Pause ~1 second after every stop before the next input.** Camera follow is
   lerped at 0.09 and needs about a second to settle. *Exception:* DOM overlays
   (memory table, dialogs) do not wait on the camera — you may press `E` the
   moment the prompt appears.
6. Player speed is **215 px/s**, normalised on diagonals. All hold times below
   are derived from it. Interaction radius is **92 px**
   (`INTERACTION_DISTANCE`, `sandboxScene.ts:7`).
7. **Do not walk east along y ≈ 770.** The pond collider ends at y = 765 and the
   player's foot body is only ~6 px clear. The route below uses y ≈ 794.
8. **Residents move now. Do not fight it.** See the drift note in §4.
9. **DOM focus does not block movement.** After the sound-controls beat (shot 6)
   keyboard focus sits on a sidebar control, not the world. Phaser registers
   the movement keys on its **window-level** keyboard plugin (`configureInput()`,
   `sandboxScene.ts:293-308`; no `input.keyboard.target` override in the game
   config at `sandboxScene.ts:891-908`), so `W`/`A`/`S`/`D` still drive the
   player. **Confirm this once in a rehearsal take** before the real take, and
   move the cursor out of frame — do **not** click the canvas to "restore
   focus", because the virtual d-pad lives there.

## 4. Shot-by-shot table

### Operator note — residents drift (new behaviour, read before shot 2)

Residents are no longer static props. Each one wanders inside a **46 px radius**
of its home point at **17 px/s**, pausing **1.4–4.6 s** between moves
(`RESIDENT_WANDER_RADIUS`, `RESIDENT_SPEED`, `sandboxScene.ts:90-92, 686-722`).
They turn to face the player, bob as they walk, and their speech bubble
**fades in and out over 220 ms** based on proximity (**190 px**,
`BUBBLE_DISTANCE`). `updateResidents()` runs even while a dialog is open, so
they keep drifting behind the overlay.

Consequences for filming:

- **The interact prompt is anchored to a fixed marker, not to the resident.**
  `updateNearbyInteraction()` measures against the static `WORLD_INTERACTIONS`
  coordinates, so drift never moves the prompt. Every hold time below is still
  exact.
- **The resident may not be standing where the storyboard frame shows them.**
  That is fine. Do not re-shoot for it.
- **Speech bubbles need a beat.** A bubble is a 220 ms alpha tween triggered by
  crossing 190 px, so it may not be up the instant you stop.
- **If the interact prompt is not on screen when you land: wait ~1 second, or
  tap one movement key once. Do not re-shoot the take.** A resident's drift or
  a few pixels of camera settle is never worth a new take.
- Shots 2–4 are safe by construction: the player at (790, 342) is ~60 px from
  Uncle Ravi's home (735, 365), and **~106 px even at maximum drift** — well
  inside the 190 px bubble range. His bubble is guaranteed visible.

### The one timing trap — shot 9 into shot 10

`selectActivityChoice()` calls `renderSandboxState()` **immediately** when you
press `Enter` (`src/main.ts:241-249`), and that is what fires the dusk tween.
The 2.6 s fade therefore starts **while the dialog is still covering the
world**. Tap `Escape` about **0.6 s** after `Enter` and roughly 2.0 s of the
tween — the part where the change is most visible — plays on camera. Hold the
response text for 3 s the way you did for Mdm Siti and the estate will already
be gold when the overlay clears, which throws away the single best shot in the
film.

Nothing is lost by closing fast: Aunty Mei's response text is reproduced in the
journal, and the evening reflection quotes the herb clause verbatim in shot 11.

## 8. Post-production

```bash
ffmpeg -y -i /tmp/gameplay_raw.mp4 -i /tmp/voiceover.m4a \
  -vf "scale=1920:1080:flags=lanczos" \
  -c:v libx264 -preset slow -crf 19 -pix_fmt yuv420p \
  -c:a aac -b:a 192k -shortest -movflags +faststart \
  "Kampung SG-Game Demo Video-TheTwoGuys.mp4"
```

**Audio decision:** `screencapture -v` records video only, and this machine has
no system-audio loopback, so the gameplay track is silent. Default plan =
**narration only** (record in QuickTime → File → New Audio Recording). The game
*does* have synthesised audio, which is why shot 6 films the sound controls
rather than merely captioning them.

**Optional upgrade if ≥ 15 minutes remain: capture app audio in OBS 32.1.2.**
This is worth more in rev-2 than it was in rev-1, because two beats now have an
audible payoff that silent footage throws away: shot 6 (the Effects slider and
the mute toggle actually changing what you hear) and shot 10 (the ambience
shifting with `audio.setEveningMood(true)` under the dusk tween). If you get
app audio, duck it to about −18 dB under the narration.

**Captions:** this ffmpeg build has **no libass and no freetype** — every
`subtitles=` and `drawtext=` command will fail. Upload the SRT below to YouTube
(Subtitles → English → Upload file), and for the downloadable MP4 burn captions
in via the Chrome-transparent-PNG + `overlay` route.
